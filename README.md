# Salifort Turnover Project
Taking employee survey data from Salifort Motors Company to create a machine learning model that can predict whether an associate will leave the company.
# Overview
In order to address the issue of employee retention, the task of this project was to analyze associate data collected by the HR department of a large consulting firm and ultimately use the data to generate a machine learning model. The model would be used to identify which workers are likely to leave while highlighting the factors that contribute to turnover. By zeroing in on the most important factors, initiatives can be introduced with the goal of keeping more associates long term. Two machine learning models were trained and tested on validation data before selecting a champion model. The champion model was then retrained on training and validation data prior to its final evaluation based on unseen data. The models selected in this project were the Random Forest and XGBoost modeling techniques. The champion model returned an accuracy score of 98.3% and a recall score of 93.6%. Both models highlighted the same top 5 factors that influence turnover. Satisfaction level, average monthly hours, recent evaluation score, number of projects, and time spent with the company were ranked as the highest contributing features to whether an employee would leave the company.
# Business Understanding
The managers in human resources department of Salifort Motors are the primary stakeholders in this scenario. The processes that go into replacing a position on any level are both time consuming and costly financially. Finding, interviewing, and hiring new employees can take weeks or even months to complete. This inevitability costs the company on multiple fronts. The lack of production from the vacant position is an obvious expense. The labor costs from human resources redirecting their energy from the other tasks they have is often overlooked but can still have a substantial effect on a business's overall efficiency. These are just a few reasons why having high retention rates ultimately benefit many companies.

The table contained a number of features which included: self-reported satisfaction level, tenure, average hours per month, number of projects, salary level, the employee's department, if they were involved in a work accident, whether or not they were promoted in the last five years, and finally whether or not they left the company. # Modeling and Evaluation
Two separate modeling methodologies were pitted against each other and scored based on validation data performance. Recall score was the primary metric used to determine optimal parameters for each model because it indicates a reduction in the number of false positives which in this case means fewer workers who were predicted to stay but actually left the company. The first model was a Random Forest model that returned a recall score of 91.2% and an accuracy of 98.2% when tested on validation data. The second and ultimate champion model was a XGBoost model with a recall score of 91.4% and an accuracy of 98.1% when tested on the same validation data. Both models performed very well on validation data but based on the slightly higher recall score, the XGBoost model was chosen to complete the analysis. When trained on both training and validation data then evaluated against a separate set of test data the champion models scores were 93.6% recall and 98.3% accuracy. # Conclusion
The machine learning model in this project proved to be incredibly effective at predicting classification among employees when it comes to whether or not they will leave Salifort Motors. Satisfaction, average hours, last evaluation, and number of projects were the primary factors in determining associate turnover. A deeper look into the correlations to these variables can point towards actionable initiatives that will help the company retain more of its employees. Since satisfaction was the highest deciding factor looking at the correlations to satisfaction level showed a negative correlation with number of projects. While average hours didn't show much correlation with satisfaction there was a significant correlation with the number of projects. From that observation a recommended course of action would be to lighten project loads especially on those with the highest project counts and the most hours to prevent burnout. Fewer projects should result fewer hours as well, providing a solution to both of the top determining factors. Even though the model performed well in its current iteration, additional data such as more granular salary data, a count of the number of total promotions, time since last promotion, and additional performance review scores from prior evaluation would likely benefit model considerably. Investigating and rectifying the discrepancies in the satisfaction scores of the workers with the most hours would also provide the model with higher quality data it could use to improve performance results.
